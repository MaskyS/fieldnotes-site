## Remaining inventions and omissions in selected drawing

This is an unscaled painted map assembled from several views, not a surveyed plan or a single filmed viewpoint. Layout on the sheet, relative object sizes, the illustrative angle and the position of the uphill roof fragments are graphic decisions. The two roof fragments do not establish the number of work buildings or identify which roof is workshop versus barn. Their ridge/eave geometry, timber lines and light-colored wall triangle are synthesized. Dashed extremities indicate uncertainty, not measured architectural edges.

The house has the observed tan walls, reddish timber balcony, green panel-bearing roof, gray earth-home frontage, pool at the left of the vegetable terraces, and beds below the home. Individual stone shapes, paving joints, panel grids, balcony slats, windows, crop distribution and leaf details are painted approximations. The pool proportions and copings are approximate. The terraces are rendered more regular and block-like than the rough layered stone in the ground view; the right-hand stepped edges and exact riser heights are inferred. The hidden structure is not reconstructed, but its facade and roof still simplify geometry. The observed chimney, pool handrail, some furniture and other small equipment are omitted for map clarity, not evidence of absence. Green in the narrow directly observed house terrace is local surface context, not a surveyed vegetation map.

The sawmill is reduced to a rail and logs, omitting much of the actual green carriage mechanism; the two neat log positions and rail geometry are illustrative. Orchard branching, leaf outlines and small trunk spacing are illustrative, not individual-tree records. Pollinator blossoms remain somewhat more uniform and dense than the tiny scattered blooms in the frame. The creek still has invented individual stone placement and a clearer bank/channel than the dark, blurred film glimpse. The hay-barn roof and sheet walls follow the long low former-greenhouse exterior; its complete right gable, post spacing and fabric folds are interpretive despite nearby dashed edges. Pasture texture is generic, with a few light flecks rather than literal film grass. The pond's reeds and water shape are artistic; its total shoreline is not established, and the illustration must not be used as a pond footprint. The chimney's taper, stone joints and opening proportions are approximations. No former room/house footprint is supplied.

No human figures, parcel boundary, distances, continuous terrain, outer tree locations, creek-to-pond connection, or route joining travel edits is drawn. All seven requested outlying features have separate locator insets; sawmill is the eighth. The 360-acre extent is explicitly the uploader's claim. Josh's roughly 90% building-wood statement is explicitly attributed.

## Filmed-document check

All 27 existing contact sheets were screened for documents and plans. Six candidate document frames were extracted at native 1280×720 using the requested ffmpeg command and viewed. At 07:36 the wall map is Italy. At 28:08 and 28:15 the book shows a cover and dedication/photo page. At 29:36 the book has a roof-water collection schematic, at 29:43 prose about gravity flow and groundwater, and at 29:48 a partly obscured groundwater/well section. None establishes this property's site plan. The water schematic was therefore considered first and attached first, but not used to supply geographic positions; the drawing truthfully says the filmed book diagrams concern water systems, not site layout. A caption saying this layout followed a plan shown in the film would have been unsupported.

Times in labels round the catalogue frame times to the nearest second or use the requested narration ranges; provenance numeric times remain copied exactly.
