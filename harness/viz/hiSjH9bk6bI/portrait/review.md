# Panjab in leafy afternoon light

Round 1, `candidate-1.png`, was chosen. The user reported that round 1 was accepted with retention 1.0. No further painting or correction was made. This acceptance does not erase the visual deviations recorded in `critique-1.md`.

The painting is a gouache interpretation of the single vantage `frame-0354` at 18:14.5, with whole-subject scope. This frame was chosen because its clear, people-free elevated three-quarter view makes the converted barge's glazing, planted ledge, pale roof and raised terrace legible together. The more distant aerial diminished these details, while frame-0353 cropped the far end. Four supporting catalogue frames clarified visible materials and details; they were not intended to supply additional scenery or hidden rooms.

## What is faithful

The painting retains the main arrangement: the long barge recedes toward the upper left, its rounded white end cabin and open terrace occupy the right foreground, and leafy riverbank vegetation rises behind it. Water surrounds the hull, with a neighboring boat partially visible at upper left and a small cropped boat fragment at bottom right.

The defining visible features remain: a nearly black hull with round and elongated openings and white trim; long black-framed glazing beside a potted ledge; a pale curved roof with two rectangular rooflights and one dark flue; four visible rectangular windows in the white end cabin; and a railed terrace with seating, planters and a red lifebuoy. The leafy daylight setting is preserved. No people, sky, bridge, solar panels, exposed spa, lower-room cutaway or interior glass staircase were added.

## What is invented or changed, and why

Gouache brushwork interprets individual leaves, flowers, ripples, reflections and surface texture. These marks are not evidence of particular plant species, interior objects or hull damage. Some details become more explicit than the film supports: terrace plank joints, hull scuffs, far-deck and neighboring-boat fittings, and a reddish upright shape and pale platform behind the roof. These are unsupported elaborations of indistinct source shapes, not verified property features.

There are also unintended fidelity errors. The near end is enlarged and extends farther right and downward, altering the cabin, terrace and surrounding water proportions. Window dimensions, hull stripes, railing posts, furniture overlaps, planter spacing and small fittings drift from the vantage. The terrace table is not distinctly resolved. Foliage is brighter and yellower, water bluer and more strongly patterned, the waterline band more prominent, and atmospheric haze reduced. These differences arose in the generated painted interpretation; they were not required by the evidence or intentionally introduced to improve the property. The detailed record remains in `critique-1.md`.

Hull registration lettering and the film watermark were deliberately omitted to comply with the requested ban on text. Hidden interiors, shore access and spa details remain outside the painting because the chosen vantage does not reveal them. The accepted image is therefore a recognizable, evidence-grounded painted view with acknowledged proportional and detail limitations, rather than an exact visual measurement of the vessel.
