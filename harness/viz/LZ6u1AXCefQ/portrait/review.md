# Accepted property portrait

## Chosen round

Round 2, `candidate-2.png`, is the accepted painting. The independent judge reported retention **1.0**. Round 1 scored 0.944 on 18 questions; its sole reported failure was the roof reading as dark gray instead of gray. Round 2 lightened the metal roof while retaining the composition and object arrangement. Acceptance establishes agreement on the judged questions, not exact correspondence of every painted detail.

The title is **Jason and Sunni’s cordwood home, leaf-on**. The vantage is `frame-0038` at **01:54**, with scope **most**: the main house and its wooded setting, not the entire property. Supporting catalogue frames `frame-0090` at 05:00, `frame-0184` at 10:24 and `frame-0231` at 13:32 clarified the facing gable and material textures. They were not used to introduce hidden architecture.

## What is faithful

The painting preserves the elevated viewpoint, landscape framing, leafy season and broad proportions of the selected moment. The house remains at middle-right under its shallow gray ribbed-metal gable roof, with ochre timber posts and fascia, pale mortar containing irregular wood ends, a stone base, dark bottle patterns and the dense bottle panel at the right end of the façade. The white-framed sliding doorway remains left of the single rectangular window, with its right half opening into a dark interior.

The visible surroundings retain their arrangement: woodland behind and left of the house, the dark lidded tub and leaning materials at left, buckets and hoses near the façade, the foreground tarp-covered construction pile, and the lane curving behind the house at right. No opposite gable, interior rooms, cabin, sawmill, pond or other unseen property feature was brought into this view.

## What is invented or approximate, and why

The gouache treatment approximates individual log ends, bottle positions, stone joints, roof ribs, wood grain, glazing reflections, leaves and small ground textures. Their broad materials and patterns come from the frames, but their exact marks are generated brushwork rather than a literal tracing. Some wall patterns are more regular and the light is warmer and more contrasty than in the film.

Small objects are less exact: the dog is larger and farther left, bucket overlaps and hose shapes differ, and tarp folds, sheet edges, boards and distant objects have been simplified or made more definite than the source supports. The tiny roof-mounted object's shape is approximate; its function is not established. The accepted roof is lighter than round 1 and may remain brighter than the photographic surface. These limitations are detailed in `critique-1.md` and `critique-2.md`.

The person at lower left and the source watermark were deliberately omitted to follow the requested ban on people and text. The small area uncovered by removing the person was filled with plausible adjacent ground, so those pixels are invented. The dog was retained because it is visible in the vantage.

Round 2 targeted only the judge's roof-colour finding. The image tool also subtly regenerated fine textures outside the roof, although no clear new structural or placement change was observed. Other known discrepancies were left alone because the revision instruction required preserving everything the judge retained. The result is an accepted painted interpretation of one recorded view, not a reconstruction of unseen parts of the property.
