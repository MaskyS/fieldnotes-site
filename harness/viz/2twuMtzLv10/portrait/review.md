# Accepted property portrait

Round 2, `candidate-2.png`, is the chosen painting. The user reported acceptance with independent-judge retention **1.0**. Round 1 had reported retention 0.882 on 17 questions. Acceptance establishes agreement on the judged questions, not exact fidelity in every painted detail.

## Vantage and choice

The painting is based on `review/2twuMtzLv10/evidence/frame-0250.jpg` at **21:03.5**. This people-free, unobstructed whole-facade view includes the entry deck, lawn and fence. Frame-0107 has foreground leaves obscuring the lower entry; frame-0027 has a person in front. The ranking pooled features across nearby cuts, so its bathroom frames were not suitable exterior vantages.

Supporting catalogue frames frame-0107 (08:31.5), frame-0008 (00:21) and frame-0031 (02:14) clarified facade trim, canopy construction, stair edges, planters, lanterns and the handrail already visible in the chosen vantage. Their different camera positions, people and open doors were not imported. “Spring” in the title describes fresh foliage, not a verified filming date.

## What is faithful

The painting retains the vantage's low oblique front view and principal arrangement: white upper walls; black horizontally ribbed skirting; one large two-part window left of the closed dark entry door; two narrow windows to its right; the door wreath; two rectangular entry lanterns; a shallow canopy with two diagonal braces; two long flower boxes; entry shrubs and pots; chains at the left building corner and canopy; and the green cylindrical bird feeder on its black pole.

The timber-edged dark stairs and projecting deck, right-side stair handrail, horizontal timber fence, patchy sloping lawn, right-hand stepping stones and wooded background remain in their corresponding positions. No person, solar panel, water tank or chimney was added. The interior, hidden elevations and larger family house are not separately depicted; the faint building reflection in the left window belongs to the vantage itself.

Round 2 removes the cream cast from the upper walls so they read as white. The other reported loss, “horizontal” versus “horizontally,” was a grammatical difference describing the same rib direction. The edit preserved that direction rather than rotating or redesigning the skirting. Questions and reference answers were left unchanged.

## What is invented or approximate, and why

Gouache brush marks replace photographic texture. Individual leaves, flowers, grass strokes, wood grain, masonry joints and reflected branches are painted approximations, not separately established details. The image tool also resynthesized some fine texture outside the wall-colour edit in round 2; preservation between rounds is not pixel-exact.

Several departures exceed the intended change of medium: distinct blue-gray and cream clouds replace a nearly uniform overcast sky; the fence and planting are more saturated; trees, chains and some foliage are heavier; window reflections are more explicit and differently arranged; and the entry lanterns acquire a conspicuous warm glow. These are generation deviations, not features supported by the vantage.

Smaller differences remain in roof and canopy profiles, panel seams, corrugation highlights, door details, planter and feeder shapes, railing divisions, deck outlines, fence spacing and stepping-stone shapes. The composition is close, but it is not a measured tracing. `critique-1.md` and `critique-2.md` retain the detailed observations.

Round 2 is chosen because it corrected the wall-colour reading while preserving the judged arrangement and achieved the reported acceptance. The remaining deviations were not corrected during that round because the instruction was to address exactly the named losses and keep everything else. They are disclosed here rather than treated as evidence or concealed by the perfect retention score.
